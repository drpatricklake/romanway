## Short description

A course in the Latin language and the civilization of ancient Rome, with guided reading, purposeful practice, and a shared journey through Roman history.


## About the course

Latin: The Roman Way grows out of a long tradition of teaching the language in close connection with the civilization that spoke it. Its beginnings lie in First Year Latin, published in 1965 by Dr. Frank J. Groten, Jr. and John A. Anderson of The Hill School. The textbook series on which this app is based preserves much of the character of that original course while substantially revising and expanding its grammar, readings, and treatment of Roman history and culture.

The purpose of the course is to prepare students to read Latin with increasing accuracy, confidence, and independence. That requires a sound knowledge of forms and vocabulary, an understanding of how words work together, and the patience to attend closely to what a sentence actually says. It also requires something worth reading. Throughout the course, Latin sentences and connected passages introduce students to the stories, ideas, conflicts, and institutions of the Roman world.

The app extends this work by giving students more opportunities to practice and more immediate information about their progress. A student who has not yet mastered a concept can return to it in several different ways. A student who is ready to move further can find additional readings and challenges. Instructors can review the work their students have completed and adjust the expectations of the course to the needs of their classes.

The Roman road gives the course its central metaphor. As students advance through the lessons, their study of the language accompanies a journey through Roman history. Along the way, the Forum, the Circus, the Arena, and the other spaces of the app give different kinds of work a place of their own. This arrangement keeps the main sequence of instruction manageable while making further practice readily available.

The games, personae, and rewards have an educational purpose. Repeated encounters with a word or form are essential to learning it; variety can make that repetition more inviting. The Sentence Lab asks students to explain how a sentence works. The Atrium gives them opportunities to help one another understand it. Progress thus includes the habits of careful study and the willingness to contribute to the learning of others.

The Romans themselves remain the subject of serious inquiry. Their literature and history reveal a civilization that was often deeply foreign to our own, capable of extraordinary achievement and profound injustice. Students should encounter that world honestly, with enough knowledge to understand it and enough freedom to question it. I hope this course will help them discover the grandeur, beauty, and utility of Latin, and prepare them to approach its literature with both sympathy and judgment.


## Features and their purpose

### The Roman road

The main road connects the lessons and gives students a clear sense of where they are going. Grammatical checkpoints provide occasions to consolidate what has been learned before taking on more demanding work.

### The lesson workspace

Instruction, guided notes, vocabulary, and essential exercises belong together. The lesson workspace keeps the central work of each lesson accessible without requiring students to pass through every optional activity.

### The Sentence Lab

Reading Latin requires attention to relationships among words. The Sentence Lab breaks the work into manageable steps so that students can identify the structure of a sentence and support their translations with an understanding of its grammar.

### Vocabulary and derivatives

Vocabulary practice gives students repeated opportunities to recognize meanings, identify forms, and recall principal parts. Cognates and derivatives connect their study of Latin with the English words they already use.

### The Codex and Bibliothēca

The Codex gathers grammatical references and notes as the course develops. The Bibliothēca offers further reading, giving students occasions to use what they have learned beyond the exercises of the current lesson.

### The Circus and Arena

Races and quiz contests give familiar material a different setting. The Circus encourages quick recall; the Arena provides another occasion to test accuracy and review mistakes. Both draw their educational value from the Latin practiced and the feedback that follows.

### The Atrium and Forum

The Atrium gives students a place to ask questions and explain difficult material to classmates. Shared projects in the Forum connect individual progress with contributions to a larger community.

### Personae and progress

Roman callings, companions, and accessories give students choices as they advance through the course. Dēnāriī provide a spendable reward, while the virtue tracks recognize different kinds of effort and contribution. These systems are intended to sustain interest over a long course of study.

### The instructor’s role

Instructors can see student progress, assign work, and set expectations for further practice. The app supplies information that can help teachers decide when a student needs encouragement, a different explanation, or a greater challenge.

### How the app was developed

I have developed the app through a process of curriculum design, software development, editorial review, and classroom testing. AI tools, including ChatGPT/Codex, Claude, and Gemini, have assisted with programming, artwork, editorial work, or design planning. I direct the app’s educational design and am responsible for reviewing the material and deciding what is included.

The app continues to develop in response to classroom use. A mistaken answer key, an unclear explanation, or a technical problem can interfere with learning. Reports from students and instructors help identify such problems and guide subsequent revisions.
